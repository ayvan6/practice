History

The US Dollar was established by the Coinage Act of 1792. It was initially backed by gold and silver (bimetallic standard). The Federal Reserve was created in 1913 to manage monetary policy.

In 1944, the Bretton Woods Agreement established the dollar as the world's reserve currency, with other currencies pegged to it. When President Nixon ended dollar-to-gold convertibility in 1971, the USD became a fiat currency.

Denominations

Coins: 1¢ (penny), 5¢ (nickel), 10¢ (dime), 25¢ (quarter), 50¢ (half dollar), $1

Banknotes: $1, $2, $5, $10, $20, $50, $100

Why USD is Important

  • World's primary reserve currency
  • Used in ~88% of international forex transactions
  • Oil and many commodities are priced in USD
  • Many countries peg their currency to the dollar
  • Considered a "safe haven" during economic uncertainty
💡 Fun Fact: The $ symbol may have evolved from the abbreviation "PS" for peso, with the S gradually written over the P.

Interesting Facts

  • All US bills are printed on paper made of 75% cotton and 25% linen
  • The average lifespan of a $1 bill is about 6 years
  • The phrase "In God We Trust" has appeared on all US currency since 1957
