🇯🇵 Japanese Yen (JPY)

History

The Yen was introduced in 1871 during the Meiji government's modernization efforts, replacing the complex Tokugawa-era monetary system. The name "yen" means "circle" or "round object."

After World War II, the yen was pegged to the US Dollar at 360 yen per dollar (1949–1971). It became a free-floating currency in 1973.

Denominations

Coins: ¥1, ¥5, ¥10, ¥50, ¥100, ¥500

Banknotes: ¥1,000, ¥5,000, ¥10,000 (new designs released in 2024)

💡 Fun Fact: The ¥5 coin has a hole in the center and is considered lucky. Its pronunciation "go-en" (五円) sounds like the word for "good fortune/connection."

Why JPY is Important

  • Third most traded currency in forex markets
  • Considered a "safe haven" currency during market turmoil
  • Japan has the world's third-largest economy
  • Used in carry trade strategies (borrowing yen to invest elsewhere)

Unique Characteristics

  • No subunits — you can't have half a yen
  • Very low interest rates for decades
  • Japan is a mostly cash-based society despite technological advancement
  • The ¥500 coin is one of the highest-value coins in circulation worldwide
