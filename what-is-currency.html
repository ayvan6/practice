What is Currency?

Currency is a system of money in general use within a particular country or economic context. It serves as a medium of exchange, a unit of account, and a store of value.

Three Functions of Currency

  1. Medium of Exchange — Currency eliminates the need for barter by providing a universally accepted item for trade.
  2. Unit of Account — It provides a standard measure to price goods and services.
  3. Store of Value — Currency can be saved and retrieved later while maintaining its purchasing power.

Characteristics of Good Currency

  • Durability — Must withstand physical wear
  • Portability — Easy to carry and transfer
  • Divisibility — Can be broken into smaller units
  • Uniformity — Each unit is identical
  • Acceptability — Widely recognized and accepted
  • Limited Supply — Scarcity maintains value
💡 Did You Know? The word "currency" comes from the Latin word "currens," meaning "to run" or "to flow," reflecting how money circulates through an economy.

Currency vs. Money

While often used interchangeably, money is a broader concept that includes anything accepted as payment. Currency specifically refers to the physical or digital tokens (coins, banknotes, digital units) issued by a government or authority.
