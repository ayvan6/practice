📜 History of Currency

The Barter System (Before 3000 BCE)

Before currency existed, people traded goods directly. A farmer might exchange wheat for a potter's bowls. This system had major limitations — what if the potter didn't need wheat?

Commodity Money (3000 BCE – 600 BCE)

Societies began using commodities with intrinsic value as money:

  • Cattle and livestock
  • Cowrie shells (used in Africa, Asia, Oceania)
  • Salt (origin of the word "salary")
  • Grain and barley

Metal Coins (600 BCE)

The first official coins were minted in Lydia (modern-day Turkey) around 600 BCE. Made from electrum (gold-silver alloy), they carried a lion's head stamp guaranteeing weight and purity.

💡 Fun Fact: The Chinese independently developed coins around the same time, initially shaped like tools (knives, spades).

Paper Money (7th Century CE)

China's Tang Dynasty introduced the first paper money called "flying cash" — deposit receipts that merchants could exchange for coins in different cities. The Song Dynasty later issued official government notes.

The Gold Standard (19th Century)

Countries tied their currencies to a fixed amount of gold. The British pound was the dominant global currency under this system.

Fiat Currency (1971 – Present)

When the United States ended the gold standard in 1971, currencies became "fiat" — backed by government decree rather than physical commodities.

Digital & Cryptocurrency (2009 – Present)

Bitcoin launched in 2009, introducing decentralized digital currency. Today, thousands of cryptocurrencies exist alongside central bank digital currencies (CBDCs).
