🇬🇧 British Pound Sterling (GBP)

PropertyDetails
Symbol£
ISO CodeGBP
Subunit1 Pound = 100 Pence
Central BankBank of England
First Issued~775 AD
NicknameQuid

History

The British Pound is the oldest currency still in use. Its origins trace back to Anglo-Saxon England, when a pound of silver was divided into 240 silver pennies (called "sterling").

The Bank of England, established in 1694, is one of the world's oldest central banks. Britain adopted the gold standard in 1821 and abandoned it in 1931.

Denominations

Coins: 1p, 2p, 5p, 10p, 20p, 50p, £1, £2

Banknotes: £5, £10, £20, £50 (Bank of England also issues larger denominations for internal use)

💡 Fun Fact: UK banknotes feature the reigning monarch. Notes featuring King Charles III entered circulation in 2024.

Why GBP is Important

  • Fourth most traded currency globally
  • London is one of the world's largest financial centers
  • Historically was the world's reserve currency before USD
  • British territories and dependencies also use GBP or pegged currencies

Interesting Facts

  • The £ symbol comes from the letter "L" for Latin "libra" (pound)
  • UK polymer banknotes are waterproof and more durable than paper
  • Scotland and Northern Ireland print their own banknotes (valid across UK)
