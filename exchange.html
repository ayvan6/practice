💹 Exchange Rates

An exchange rate is the value of one currency expressed in terms of another. For example, if USD/INR = 83, then 1 US Dollar equals 83 Indian Rupees.

Types of Exchange Rate Systems

1. Floating (Flexible) Exchange Rate

Currency value determined by market supply and demand. Most major currencies (USD, EUR, GBP, JPY) use this system.

2. Fixed (Pegged) Exchange Rate

Currency value tied to another currency or basket. Examples: Hong Kong Dollar (pegged to USD), some Gulf currencies.

3. Managed Float

Mostly market-driven, but central bank intervenes occasionally. China's Yuan operates this way.

Factors Affecting Exchange Rates

  • Interest Rates — Higher rates attract foreign investment
  • Inflation — Lower inflation typically strengthens currency
  • Economic Performance — Strong GDP growth attracts capital
  • Political Stability — Uncertainty weakens currency
  • Trade Balance — Trade surplus strengthens currency
  • Government Debt — High debt can weaken currency
💡 Key Terms:
Appreciation — Currency gains value
Depreciation — Currency loses value
Devaluation — Government deliberately lowers currency value

Reading Exchange Rates

Exchange rates are quoted in pairs:

  • EUR/USD = 1.10 means 1 Euro = 1.10 US Dollars
  • Base currency = First currency (EUR)
  • Quote currency = Second currency (USD)

Bid vs. Ask Price

TermMeaning
BidPrice at which you can sell the base currency
AskPrice at which you can buy the base currency
SpreadDifference between bid and ask (bank's profit)

Why Exchange Rates Matter

  • Affects cost of imports and exports
  • Impacts international travel expenses
  • Influences foreign investment decisions
  • Affects inflation and purchasing power
