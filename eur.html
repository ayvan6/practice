History

The Euro was introduced electronically on January 1, 1999, and physical banknotes and coins entered circulation on January 1, 2002. It replaced national currencies like the German Mark, French Franc, and Italian Lira.

The Euro is managed by the European Central Bank (ECB), headquartered in Frankfurt, Germany.

Eurozone Countries (2024)

Austria, Belgium, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain.

Denominations

Coins: 1c, 2c, 5c, 10c, 20c, 50c, €1, €2

Banknotes: €5, €10, €20, €50, €100, €200, €500 (€500 no longer issued but still valid)

💡 Design Note: Euro banknotes feature architectural styles from different European periods (Classical, Romanesque, Gothic, etc.) — but the buildings are fictional to avoid favoring any country.

Why EUR is Important

  • Second most traded currency globally
  • Second largest reserve currency after USD
  • Used by over 340 million people daily
  • Facilitates trade within the EU single market

Interesting Facts

  • The € symbol was inspired by the Greek letter epsilon (Ε) and the first letter of "Europe"
  • Each country designs its own national side for coins
  • The ECB sets a single interest rate for the entire Eurozone
